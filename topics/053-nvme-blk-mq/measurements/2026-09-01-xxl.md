## Fixed campaign

Each timed process ran one userspace thread and exactly 8,192 verified 4 KiB
`O_DIRECT` reads, or 33,554,432 bytes. A private 128 MiB file was initialized,
synced, and verified before the campaign. Linux AIO setup happened before the
timed interval; `io_destroy` happened after it. The timed region covered the
submission/completion loop and data checks.

The depth campaign used eight complete four-process blocks in the fixed order
`ABBA BAAB ABBA BAAB BAAB ABBA BAAB ABBA`. `A` was depth one and `B` was
depth eight. Each letter launched a fresh process. One whole block, not an
individual read or process, was the analysis unit. The fixed horizon was 32
processes and eight block contrasts.

| Application depth | Processes | IOPS minimum | IOPS median | IOPS maximum |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 16 | 1,741.235 | 1,749.317 | 1,759.325 |
| 8 | 16 | 8,654.344 | 13,916.278 | 14,044.350 |

The predeclared estimate was the geometric mean of the eight whole-block log
IOPS ratios. Depth eight divided by depth one was `6.955513`, with a two-sided
95% Student-t interval of `[6.312387, 7.664163]` and 7 degrees of freedom.
The depth-eight block ratios ranged from `6.297372` to `8.014782`; that
variation is part of the accepted result, not a basis for selective deletion.
The interval describes between-block variation in this exact host, source,
binary, kernel, filesystem, block stack, and run window. It does not include
host-population, device-population, or rebuild variation.

## Integrity and code-generation checks

All 64 timed attempts were valid and used 64 unique process identifiers.
Every process reported one thread before and after timing, `pid == tid`, zero
errors, 8,192 verified reads, and 33,554,432 process-accounted read bytes.
Every `STATX_DIOALIGN` query succeeded and reported 4-byte memory alignment and
512-byte offset alignment; the probe used an 8-byte allocation alignment.
Every depth-one process reached `peak_outstanding=1`; every depth-eight process
reached `peak_outstanding=8`. The direct-mode schema's residency fields were
zero sentinels; they do not establish page-cache residency.

The device-wide `nvme0n1` completed-read delta ranged from 8,192 to 9,267
during individual process windows. These counters can include ambient host
traffic and are not additive across the partition and parent device.

Retained x86-64 disassembly shows `submit_all` loading syscall number 209
(`0xd1`) and executing `call <syscall@plt>` for `io_submit`. The direct
completion loop loads syscall number 208 (`0xd0`) and executes
`call <syscall@plt>` for `io_getevents`. This is the userspace call boundary
into the C library's syscall wrapper. It does not show the wrapper's eventual
trap, blk-mq dispatch, an NVMe command, or a device completion.

## Evidence boundary

Measured: raising the probe's maximum outstanding Linux AIO reads from one to
eight raised application IOPS by the reported ratio on this exact file path and
run window. The workload integrity, one-thread condition, and A/A gates passed.

Inferred: the result is consistent with greater application-level overlap
keeping more work available to the observed path than depth one did. The
source of the larger between-block variation at depth eight was not isolated.

Not established: `peak_outstanding=8` does not prove eight requests occupied a
blk-mq hardware context or one NVMe submission queue. A Linux AIO event is not
an NVMe completion-queue entry. The experiment does not isolate filesystem,
blk-mq, driver, hypervisor, EBS service, controller, cache, or media time. The
guest's NVMe name and PCIe transport do not establish local flash.
