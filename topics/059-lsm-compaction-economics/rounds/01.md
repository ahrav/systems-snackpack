# Round 1: visibility and sustainable work

For a service accepting 10 MiB/s of updates, a write is both a visible change and
future compaction work. MiB means 1,048,576 bytes. These examples use assumed
uncompressed sizes, not observed engine counters.

A job reads 64 MiB incoming plus 256 MiB overlap and writes 288 MiB after dropping
32 MiB. Total transfer is 608 MiB. At an assumed aggregate transfer service of
400 MiB/s, the ideal transfer-only bound is 1.52 seconds. It excludes computation,
queueing, and contention. The flush-plus-job SST write ratio is (64+288)/64=5.5.
It excludes WAL, later jobs, and device-internal writes. The denominator is update
bytes, not net growth in the live database.

With eight compaction transfer bytes generated per update byte, 10 MiB/s of
updates generate 80 MiB/s of work. Service of 60 MiB/s grows debt by 20 MiB/s.
A debt limit of 1,000 MiB is reached from 400 MiB in 30 seconds. Service of
100 MiB/s instead drains that 400 MiB in 20 seconds while ingestion continues.
Threshold increases buy time; they do not change sustainable capacity. The model
assumes arrivals are available for service within each interval, not an exact
schedule. An engine's estimated pending-compaction bytes may use different units.

If current retained files total 1,024 MiB, output adds 288 MiB and concurrent
writes add 64 MiB, peak space is 1,376 MiB before other retention. Current files
already include the input. Readers, checkpoints, backups, and concurrent jobs
can retain additional files.

Leveled compaction constrains overlapping runs but rewrites destination data.
Tiered compaction groups runs to reduce repeated rewriting but retains more read
candidates and may need large temporary outputs. Actual overlapping ranges,
update concentration, snapshots, compression, and file boundaries determine the
cost. Neither policy is uniformly faster. Filters can reduce absent point-lookup
work but do not reclaim versions or discharge compaction debt.

The account history is value 100 at sequence 10, value 120 at 20, deletion at 30.
A snapshot at 15 requires value 100. At floor 30, a tombstone remains necessary
if an older value survives outside the input. With complete older-version
coverage, all three records can disappear. These are visibility conditions,
not age-based expiration or distributed-repair rules.

## Evidence boundary

Both initial Linux examples passed before the topic worktree was created.
The committed-source campaign runs seven tests, one doctest, the example, and
native code-generation inspection on each required host. Timing the model does
not estimate engine throughput. No performance comparison or ISA claim is made.
Final identity and results are recorded under measurements/ after execution.

A production comparison must start from equivalent populated databases, hold
memory, compression, durability and updates fixed, balance policy order across
fresh processes, and count endpoint debt. Record per-process dispersion, read
latency, transfer bytes, peak space and stalls. Separate startup from sustained
operation. Do not mistake drained versus undrained runs for equal work.
